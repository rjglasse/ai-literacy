How This Course Works

This course is a little different. Before we get into AI, it's worth understanding how the course itself is built — because the two things are related.

This course updates itself

Artificial intelligence moves fast. By the time most courses about AI are written, reviewed, approved, and delivered, parts of them are already out of date. New tools appear. Capabilities shift. Last year's headline becomes this year's baseline.

So this course is designed in two parts:

Some things here won't change much. The big questions — what intelligence means, how learning systems actually work, how to think critically about what AI can and can't do — these are durable. We've built those parts carefully, and they'll hold up.

Some things here are written fresh for you. The current state of AI tools, the latest case studies, what's happening in policy and industry right now — these parts are generated close to when you take this course, using AI itself. What you read in those sections reflects the world as it is at your moment of study, not ours.

You'll see markers throughout telling you which is which.

Why that matters

This isn't just a technical trick. It's the first thing about AI literacy worth knowing: the field doesn't stand still, and neither should your approach to understanding it. One of the most important skills you'll develop here is knowing how to keep learning when the ground keeps moving.

How learning works here

This course doesn't work like a textbook. You won't read long sections and then answer questions at the end. Instead, you'll be asked questions before explanations — sometimes before you could possibly know the answer. That's intentional.

Getting something wrong and then finding out why is one of the most effective ways to learn. The wrong guess makes the right answer stick. So when you see a question before any context, that's not a mistake. Sit with it, commit to a guess, and then see what happens.

You'll also be asked to reflect — not for a grade, but because writing what you think helps you figure out what you actually think.

Activity: Map Your AI Starting Point

Write three examples of AI you used this week. For each, note what you think it was trying to optimise (speed, relevance, convenience, or something else) and one risk you noticed.
